  Ten months is enough, with three people. One person has 10 × 21 days minus about 12 public
  holidays, so 198 working days. Three people give 594 days against 396 of work: 67% loaded, which
  leaves room for the sick days, the rework and the two weeks a bank's review always takes.

  Two people do not fit. Two give 396 days against 396 needed, which is 100% loaded. That is every
  day booked, no illness, no rework, nothing late. Any plan at that number slips in month three.

  If it has to be two people, 100 days of scope come out. The cut list is on the last page, and each
  line is one a bank will ask about.

Two things ten months does not buy

The nine items that are not build tasks. A Gartner Magic Quadrant listing, PCI-DSS and GDPR
certification, concurrent licensing, certified implementation partners, onsite training, a 3-year OEM
warranty and a 7-year AMC. These need a commercial decision and an auditor, and they run in
parallel from month one, not at the end. PCI-DSS assessment alone typically takes three to six
months of calendar time with an external assessor, so it starts in month two or it is late.

Carrier-grade capacity. The plan proves 500 agents and 1,000 ports on our own stack with a
load harness. Proving it on the bank's hardware, under their network, signed off by their own
team, is their environment and their timeline.

The three rules that keep this plan honest

1. A line is done when a test asserts it, not when it demonstrates. That is why 52 of the 396
days are testing and hardening, and why they are not at the end only.

2. Month one measures Bangla accuracy. Both proposals lead with a number nobody has
measured. Five days, and it decides what can be promised for the other nine months.

3. The mock bank stays until a bank signs. Every integration is written against a system we
control, so nothing in this plan waits on the bank's core banking team. When they sign, the mock
is replaced and nothing above the adapter changes.
